SLIDE 1 · title

Opening

Hi, Mariano is not here, so I'll do the summary for Fetters, Curry, and Creswell 2013. I'll keep this to about three minutes.

One-line version: mixed methods only works if you actually integrate the two strands. Collecting surveys and interviews is not enough by itself.

SLIDE 2 · argument

Core Argument

The paper's main claim is that integration is what makes mixed methods valuable.

They say it happens at three levels:

1. Design: how the study is structured up front.

2. Methods: how the datasets get linked while you collect and analyze.

3. Interpretation and reporting: how you put the results together for the reader.

Their contribution is practical: concrete strategies at each level, not just “use both methods.”

SLIDE 3 · method

How They Frame It

This is a methods paper. They review designs and name the moves.

Three basic designs: exploratory sequential (qual then quant), explanatory sequential (quant then qual), and convergent (both at once, then compare).

Four ways to link data: connecting, building, merging, embedding.

Three ways to report: narrative, data transformation, joint display.

Do not list every advanced framework. Stay on those three lists.

SLIDE 4 · takeaways

Essential Findings

The takeaway I care about is fit, or how the two strands relate once you put them together.

Confirmation: they agree.

Expansion: they each add something different.

Discordance: they conflict. That can open a new question instead of killing the study.

And a joint display is just one concrete reporting tool: put the numbers and the themes in the same table so the integration is visible.

SLIDE 5 · HCI

Why This Matters Here

HCI already mixes interviews with surveys, task times, and logs.

The paper still hits: having both is not the same as connecting them.

For our projects: decide early where the integration happens, and say what the combination taught you that neither strand gave alone.

SLIDE 6 · close

Limitation + Question

One limitation: this is a principles paper. It shows what good integration looks like, but it does not measure how often people actually do it well.

Open question for the room: in an HCI study with interviews and logs, where should the integration happen first: in the design, in the analysis, or on the page when you report?
