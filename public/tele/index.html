If spatial hypertext was never defined tightly, what would count as evidence that the work has gone quiet, rather than just showing up under another name?

Answer

Quiet would mean people stopped arranging notes so the layout itself carries meaning. A new name does not count. If a tool still lets placement mean something, that is the same practice. Dormant fits better if the only current public tools are two Mac apps and most papers only mention the idea in passing.

Context

Page 107. He quotes Seven Hypertexts that spatial hypertext has become dormant. Dormant means the research has gone quiet. He says the problem is not enough tools, and no clear place to put the practice into work people already do. There was never one short definition, and he says that looseness helped. The Itinerary of King John was not built as spatial hypertext, but he says it potentially is.

If a model starts suggesting how the notes should be grouped, who is making sense of the material, and when is that suggestion too early?

Answer

The person is still making sense of it if they can ignore the suggestion. It gets too early when the model names the groups before anyone has arranged the notes. That locks a structure before you know what the notes are for. VIKI's rule was the person goes first.

Context

Pages 104 and 107. Premature formalisation means committing to a structure before you understand the problem. In VIKI the person lays the notes out first. A parser, a program that tries to recognize structure from the layout, only suggests groups if you ask. It does not create the layout. On page 107 he says language models might talk to these tools. He does not test that here.

If the map mostly works for the person who built it, should spatial hypertext stay a private thinking tool?

Answer

It can stay private while you are still figuring the problem out. That is the point of leaving the structure loose. It should not stay private if someone else is supposed to read the result. Then you need a form you can hand over, and he says the field only got part of the way there.

Context

Pages 105 to 107. He asks if the result is a mind palace only the maker can use. Shipman said the maker and the reader are often the same people, so familiarity covers the ambiguity. A general reader does not have that. VKB never really solved how to hand someone a finished piece. Tinderbox gets part of the way there.

Where would leaving the structure loose help a team, and where would it hide a decision everyone else needs to see?

Answer

Loose helps while the team is still sorting what the problem is, because naming the structure too soon locks a guess. It hides a decision when the group has already chosen something and the map still looks open, so other people cannot see what was decided.

Context

Page 107. He says the strength is exploring a problem and drawing out its shape before the structure is settled. He also says there is no clear place to build that practice into work people already do. Tinderbox and Storyspace are the only two current public tools he names, and both are Mac only.
