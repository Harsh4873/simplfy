Slide 1

Domain Experts, Design Novices

Howdy. This paper looks at what people bring to design when they already know the community but are new to design methods. It focuses on participatory design, where the people affected by a problem help shape the solution.

The main argument is that community knowledge matters throughout that process. Knowing the people and understanding their everyday lives can shape how a workshop runs and what comes out of it. That's the idea I want to focus on.

Slide 2

A year with four community teams

The researchers followed 15 facilitators across four teams for about a year. These teams worked in libraries, schools, a community college, and a community organization. They were developing resources to help people deal with misinformation.

The researchers used surveys, group interviews, and some workshop observations. They looked for patterns in how facilitators described and carried out their work. The teams also had design guidance and support. That matters because the study is about how people use their community experience while learning to lead design.

Slide 3

Community knowledge shaped the design

The authors connect community and shared culture to four other values. The first is trust. Existing relationships helped people speak openly, including disagreeing with an idea.

The second is learning together. Participants brought knowledge that helped change the work. Their role went beyond reacting to something the facilitator had already decided.

The third is respect and shared influence. Facilitators tried to make room for the community's ways of doing things. One group built its design activities into a community event, which made participation fit that setting.

Finally, they wanted solutions people could keep using. The work had to fit everyday routines. Across these values, the authors show how knowing a community can shape the design process itself.

Slide 4

What this means for participatory design

The authors suggest two ways to support people who are new to leading design. First, be open about what you don't know. In one example, a facilitator made a mistake, accepted a participant's correction, and changed a later activity. That shows what it can look like to let feedback affect the work.

Second, give communities room to participate in ways that fit them. A design session can take a different form depending on who is involved.

For HCI, this means taking community expertise seriously when deciding who can lead design and how participation should happen. These are lessons from this study, with room for more research.

Slide 5

The missing participant perspective

The main limitation is whose perspective we hear. The study tells us a lot about the people leading the workshops, but it doesn't include the participants' own accounts. The researchers observed some sessions, which helps, but we still have a limited picture of how participants experienced the process.

That leaves me with this question: how would we know participants had a real say? I would want to hear from them and see how their input changed a decision. That's what would help connect the facilitators' intentions to the participants' experience.
