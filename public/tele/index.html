Tue 10-20 primary summarizer

Script for Ko et al., Domain Experts, Design Novices
For you only. Not for the projector.
SLIDE 1 · paper

Domain Experts, Design Novices

Hello guys, my name is Harsh Dave. I am summarizing today's paper for us. It is Domain Experts, Design Novices, by Ko and colleagues, from CHI last year.

Here is the setup. The people running the design sessions were not designers. They were people who already worked in these communities. Design was the part they were still learning.

The line on the slide is from L3, a rural library facilitator. Small libraries that already feel like the same kind of place. That shared feeling is what let people say the hard stuff out loud.

SLIDE 2 · method

A year with four community teams

They followed 15 facilitators for a year, August 2022 to July 2023. Four teams, and you can see all four on the cards.

One thing the cards hide. L1, on the rural libraries team, is a graduate student. So that group is researchers, not five professors.

Most of them had only read about this kind of design. Each team had one person with a little training, plus a guide they were allowed to bend.

One of the authors sat in on a session for three of the four teams. They stayed out of the community organization event, because outsiders would have felt out of place.

SLIDE 3 · findings

Five values, in session order

They came away with five values, and the order matters. Shared culture first. Then building things together. Then respect, then trust. The last one, a solution the community keeps, only works if the rest are already true.

The librarians already shared the job and the duty to their towns, so hard topics were sayable. People in the room corrected the facilitators, and the authors treat that as a good sign.

The picture I want you to keep is Disinformation Day. That group skipped the normal workshop. Vendors, food, yoga, and that was the design session.

SLIDE 4 · strategies

Name that you are new to design

The authors pull two moves out of this. First, say you are new. L4, on the slide, used the wrong term at the kickoff and owned it in front of everyone.

Second, let the session look like their event. Disinformation Day is the example.

And the point for us. The person running this does not have to be a design researcher. The job you already have, and the fact that you belong there, is doing part of the work.

SLIDE 5 · limit

We heard the facilitators, not the room

The limit is who is missing. They heard the facilitators. They did not really hear the people who showed up. The authors say that makes the analysis weaker.

So here is my question for us. In a project like ours, what would we have to drop, so the session looks like their event instead of our agenda?

That is my summary. Handing off to the discussion.
