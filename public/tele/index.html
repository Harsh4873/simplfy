Script for Ko et al., Domain Experts, Design Novices
For you only. Not for the projector.
SLIDE 1

Open

Hello guys. I am here to summarize today's paper. It's Ko and colleagues, Domain Experts, Design Novices, from CHI last year.

Usually the person running a session like this is trained as a designer. In this paper, the people running the sessions already lived in the community. Design was the part they were still figuring out.

The quote on the slide is from L3, one of the rural library facilitators. They're talking about small libraries that already feel like the same kind of place. That shared feeling is what let people say the hard stuff out loud.

SLIDE 2

Who ran it

They followed 15 facilitators from August 2022 to July 2023. Four communities, and you can see them on the cards.

One thing that card doesn't make obvious: L1, on the rural libraries team, is a graduate student. So that group is researchers, not five professors.

Most of them had only read about this kind of design before. Each team had one person with a little training, and a guide they were allowed to bend. One of the authors sat in on a session for three of the teams. They stayed out of the community organization event, because outside researchers would have felt out of place.

SLIDE 3

What they found

They came away with five values, and the order is the point. You need a shared culture before people will build something together. Then respect, then trust. Only after that do you get something the community will actually keep.

The librarians already had the job and the duty to their towns, so the hard topics were sayable. People in the room would correct the facilitators, and the authors treat that as a good sign. The librarians tried their quizzes on their own staff. The teachers kept rewriting assignments until they fit the class.

The picture I want you to have is Disinformation Day. That group did not run a normal workshop. Vendors, food, yoga, and that was the design session. A lot of the trust was already there, because these people had worked together for years.

SLIDE 4

What to do with it

The authors pull two moves out of this for anyone who is new to it. First, just say you are new. L4, on the slide, used the wrong term at the kickoff and owned it. He told the librarians their correction showed he didn't know the community, and that's why they were in the room.

Second, let the session look like their event. Disinformation Day is the example.

For us, you don't have to be a design researcher to run this. The job you already have, and the fact that you belong there, is doing part of the work.

SLIDE 5

The limit

The limit I want to leave you with is this. They heard the facilitators. They did not really hear the people who showed up. The authors left those folks out to protect their time, and they know that makes the analysis weaker.

So the question on the slide is the one I want us on. In a project like ours, what would we have to drop so the session looks like their event, not like our agenda?

That's my summary.
